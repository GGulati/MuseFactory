# Onboarding Script

Interview order for a new deal-hunter user. Ask what unblocks; infer the rest from their data. Keep questions tight — one topic at a time.

## 1. Sizes

Tops, pants (waist + inseam, and which inseam they prefer), jackets/suits, shoe size by brand/last. Note conversions explicitly — shoe sizing varies by brand, so record the variance.

## 2. Price ceilings

By category: casual shirts, nicer shirts, casual pants, nicer pants, blazers, suits, footwear. Also ask what discount depth they expect (e.g. 50–70% off typical retail).

## 3. Fabric and fit rules

Natural-fiber minimums (e.g. 95%+ with a small elastane allowance), fabrics to always exclude, construction standards (Goodyear-welted, full-grain leather for footwear).

## 4. Brand map

Liked brands, excluded brands, and policy notes: house-brand stance, fast-fashion stance, label exceptions (e.g. "never Polo"). Used/secondhand stance: default to new-on-deep-sale unless they say otherwise; any secondhand exceptions must be labeled.

## 5. Data on-ramps (offer, don't require)

Two paths that shortcut the interview by reading real shopping data:

**Purchase-history on-ramp**
- Ask which retailers they have order history with.
- Sign in with the saved-login flow (Secure Vault); never raw credentials in chat.
- Pull order history: record brand, item, size, and price actually paid, plus the order date range and any gaps in coverage (e.g. "no orders before 2020 in this account").
- Infer: real worn sizes (more reliable than self-reported), preferred brands, and true paid-price bands per category.
- If the account shows nothing retrievable (maintenance notices, missing orders), note it as a gap and retry later — don't guess.

**Favorites on-ramp**
- Ask which retailers have wishlists / saved-for-later lists.
- Sign in the same way; read the saved lists.
- Infer brand, size, and price norms from what they bookmark — saved items reveal taste even without purchases.

Both paths end with step 6.

## 6. Draft profile review

Present the assembled draft profile (sizes, ceilings, brands, exclusions) and ask for corrections. Their corrections are the verdict: apply them to the profile file immediately, then proceed. Never re-ask what they already corrected.

## 7. Watch definitions

Propose one scan per category (e.g. footwear, menswear, designer/runway) with candidate sources. Vet every source before recommending it: legitimacy, real sale cadence, shipping to the user's region. Never recommend an unvetted site.

## Minimum viable profile

When the user has no purchase history and no favorites: exact sizes + price ceilings by category + at least one liked brand. That is enough to start scans; sources come from the skill's starter suggestions, vetted before use.
